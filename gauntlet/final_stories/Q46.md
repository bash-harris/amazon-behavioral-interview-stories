# Q46 - a time when your project deadline was very near, but you still took time to verify or test the data/code before submission

**LP:** Insist on the Highest Standards (primary); Deliver Results; Earn Trust (the reports came from users).   **Story:** the two bugs that reached users in the board-overlay tool - layer crop only working after a manual slide, saved layer position squished to the image boundary - recorded in the repo's Issues.txt, and the verify-reported-failures checklist they made me adopt before every submission (PCBvsLayoutnew/Issues.txt:1-2); distinct from Q81 (the pre-submission performance audit) and Q88 (owning fault for false alarms)

## Core Answer

Two bugs reached users in my board-overlay tool - it lays a circuit board's design layer over a photo of the real board - and they became my standing pre-submission plan.

The window was short. The tool drags the design layer onto the board photo so the automated crop inspection works on the matched area. On my machine it passed - I tested the way I designed: align, save, reopen, all inside the image. I shipped inside the date, which felt like speed and was actually luck. [PARTIAL - no dates in the file; the near-deadline is my account]

Then the reports arrived as plain notes in the project's Issues.txt file. First: the AI detection only worked if the layer was slid slightly into place, because the crop wasn't fitting correctly - the report asked for a robust way to move the layer over the board image. Second: a saved layer position always came back squished to the boundary of the image, even when the layer had legitimately been sitting partly outside the photo. Neither note records who wrote it - they read like the users of the tool, and I won't quote a person. [GAP - reporter unnamed in the record]

Both had one shape: user-visible interactions - sliding, saving, restoring - I'd treated as polish and never checked. Deadline math said skip. Users' math said the tool didn't work. So the fix round came with a rule I've kept since: before submission, run the reported failure as a named test case. I rebuilt the layer-move so the crop follows the layer, not a lucky nudge. Save now round-trips out-of-frame positions instead of clamping them to the image boundary. And the pre-submission list for anything touching overlays: slide the layer a little, slide it a lot, save with the layer partly outside the photo, close and reopen. Five minutes, stopwatch estimate, and both bug classes now have named checks. [PARTIAL - the two fixes are my account of the follow-up work; the issue file records the problems verbatim, the diffs aren't quotable]

Near deadlines stopped being a reason to skip verification and became the reason to do it: builds ship by email and USB to offline machines, a bad one spreads before I can react, and one report-diagnose-fix-redistribute cycle costs far more than five minutes up front. [PARTIAL - cost is qualitative; hours were never tracked]

## Memory Anchors
- Problem: two bugs reached users under a short window - crop only fitting after a manual layer-slide, and saved positions clamped to the image boundary - both invisible in my happy-path testing
- Customer: the engineers using the overlay tool day to day; their reports arrived as raw notes in Issues.txt, author unrecorded
- Insight: near a deadline, user-visible state transitions (drag, save, restore) are exactly what gets skipped - and exactly what ships broken
- Action (mine): rebuilt the layer-move to be robust without the nudge; fixed save/restore to keep out-of-frame positions; instituted the pre-submission checklist that runs each reported failure as a named case (~5 min, my stopwatch estimate)
- Result: the two reported classes have named checks; no instrumented before/after - the record is the issues file plus the follow-up rule [PARTIAL]
- Learning: verification time is not the budget line to cut when the date is near; a shipped bug costs a full report-diagnose-fix-redistribute cycle on offline machines
