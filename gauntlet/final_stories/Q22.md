# Q22 - tell me about a time you had a disagreement with your manager
**LP:** Have Backbone; Disagree and Commit  **Story:** [GAP] - no documented instance (framework only)

## Core Answer
I'll be honest up front: I don't have a real disagreement-with-my-manager story to tell, and I won't invent one. I built the PCB inspection suite solo, on an internship-led project, so there's no manager-blows-up scene in my record - the closest true things are stakeholder corrections I took well (an engineer telling me my BOM tool silently dropped `R1||R2` variants, and I owned that fix) and the times I committed fully to a decision I didn't pick. In plain words: the PCB inspection suite is software that photographs a finished circuit board - a PCB is the green board inside every electronic device - and checks it against the board's design file. A BOM (bill of materials) is the list of every part that belongs on a board, and my BOM tool read that list. `R1||R2` is how a design file writes "this one spot on the board can be built with either of two parts"; "silently dropped" means my tool just ignored those spots without telling anyone - an error, not a choice. A "stakeholder correction" is simply someone who uses the work telling me I got something wrong.

If you're asking how I'd handle a genuine one: I'd raise it privately, with data, once - explain the risk the way I see it and what it costs the customer - and separate the idea from the person. If the call still goes the other way, I commit to it in full and keep my mouth moving only toward making the chosen path work. "Have Backbone" and "Disagree and Commit" - the two Amazon principles this question targets - are the same coin: argue with evidence while the decision is open, then back the team's choice fully even if it wasn't yours. On this project the version I *can* speak to is the commit half: I proposed one way to parse component variants - that is, one set of rules for reading those "either/or" part notations out of the design file - got corrected on what the factory actually meant, adopted that decision completely, and then generalized past the single case they reported. That's the part I can defend for ten minutes. The part where a manager and I disagree head-to-head - I don't have a real example yet, and I'd rather say that than dress up a stakeholder correction as a boss confrontation.
