## Why it fails

1. **The rule is explicit: a framework-only answer with no documented instance is FAIL/GAP.** The answer itself states in the spoken core, "I don't have a documented event where I personally handed a colleague a piece of wrong information ... and the responsibility I take for it. It does not contain a personal wrong-information scene, and I won't claim one." That is an admission that the asked event is absent.
2. **The proxy does not meet the question.** The documented behavior is the *tool* putting wrong information in front of engineers, handled well. That genuinely evidences several Earn Trust strengths, but it is not "I gave someone wrong information," and the answer converts the interpersonal half into a generic six-step procedure. A bar raiser probing "who did you tell, what did they do before you corrected it, what did you say" gets nothing lived.
3. **Framework format breaks the STORY 4 register.** STORY 4 is an event with a constraint, technical reasoning, a measured result, and a reflection. Q25's core is a how-to list; only the pin-count paragraph behaves like a story, and it is a rule-fix, not a trust-telling.

## What is salvageable (use it if the builder revises)

- The honesty is correctly calibrated and the provenance discipline is excellent — do not trade that away for an invented scene.
- The pin-count episode is the right raw material: it is a real, owned event where the candidate's system told people something false, with a measured correction (12% → <2%) and an explicit refusal to hide the residual risk. Restructure the whole core around that single event, and demote the framework to a follow-up.
