## Prep note (how to prepare this)

This question isn't asking you to perform a story. It's asking you to do the homework that makes every other answer defensible: choose a small number of real achievements, and write down the specific details that prove them. Here's how I'd do it for this candidate.

**Pick four anchor achievements — no more.** Four deep stories beat twelve shallow ones, and each maps to several LPs at once.

1. **Shipped the PCB Inspector Suite solo — three tools in eight weeks.** The three verification steps (schematic vs EBOM, PCB vs layout, good board vs new board), unified UX, delivered and adopted by 15 engineers across two sites, about 20 hours a week saved, and the BOM tool caught three real mismatches in its first production week. Best for: **Deliver Results, Ownership, Think Big.**
2. **Root-caused the BOM parser's four edge cases.** `MP_` prefix, SAP hyperlink cells, `R1||R2` / `R1/R2` variants, and DNP positions; a 15-BOM regression suite rerun after every change; zero false positives/negatives on test, a 2,000-component BOM under 200 ms, and a 20–30-minute manual cross-reference replaced by seconds. Best for: **Dive Deep, Customer Obsession, Insist on the Highest Standards.**
3. **Replaced a server with a single offline HTML file.** ~12.5 MB of embedded libraries, base64-encoded WASM, a ~25 MB single file, ~5 s load on factory machines vs ~2 s for the server version, and localStorage auto-save with 5 MB quota handling — no install, no network, no IT ticket. Best for: **Frugality, Invent and Simplify, Bias for Action.**
4. **Built Tab Scroller, a Manifest V3 AI research workspace.** Hover ribbon and `Ctrl+K` search, an `Alt+Shift+T` natural-language command layer (Gemini + Ollama), snoozing on a `chrome.alarms` one-minute tick that survives restarts, and a token-saving engine (local rules, pre-filtered context, sub-second parallel extraction, triplet compression). Recent work extends it into workspace memory: browser-generated Markdown briefs, cross-tab Q&A with source references, evidence capture, and Markdown export. Best for: **Learn and Be Curious, Invent and Simplify, Customer Obsession.**

**Then map each anchor to the LP, and capture details the way the strong stories do.** For each one, write down: the constraint (why the obvious option was blocked), the options you actually considered, the exact number and the file it came from, the trade-off you accepted and what it cost, the measured result, and one honest "what I'd do differently." If you can't source a number, don't write it down — an unsourceable metric is the thing that collapses under a bar-raiser probe.

**Two discipline rules.** Keep one telling per story: some of the banks retell the same event with different numbers (the standalone build appears as ~25 MB and as 150 MB; the VLM appears as ~85% and as 92%), so pick one and stay consistent across answers. And don't manufacture a fifth "emotional" achievement to look well-rounded; the four above are the documented record, and they're enough.
