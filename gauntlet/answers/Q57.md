## PREP NOTE

This is a meta question: the interviewer isn't asking for a new event, they're asking how you'd construct a "proud of" answer. Use the two answers already in the bank and do not build a third.

**Primary pointer — Q9 ("proud of your work").** The canonical answer is the PCB inspection suite: three verification tools built into one suite, delivered solo in eight weeks, adopted by 15 manufacturing engineers across two sites for about 20 hours a week saved, with the BOM tool catching three real mismatches in its first production week. The pride is breadth of ownership and adoption, not a single algorithm. Lift Q9 directly.

**Alternate pointer — Q8 ("project on your own").** If the interviewer is probing solo end-to-end execution or a bold direction, use the standalone single-file build: the whole suite as one ~25MB HTML file that runs offline in about 5 seconds, replacing the server. That answer carries the Think Big "delete the architecture" decision and a clean, honest reflection.

**Which to pick:** ownership + breadth + business impact → Q9. Solo technical depth and a bold architectural call → Q8. If they say "most proud," go Q9 (the adoption outcome is the strongest close). Don't stack both; pick one and let follow-ups open the other.

**How to deliver a "proud of" answer (the shape):**

1. Lead with the outcome in one sentence — "I'm proudest that my tool stopped being my project and became the way the team worked."
2. Give the constraint and the decisions that made the outcome possible, not a feature tour.
3. Put real numbers on it: 8 weeks, 15 engineers, 2 sites, ~20 hours/week, 3 mismatches caught; or ~25MB file, ~12.5MB of embedded libraries, ~5s vs ~2s load, 8-second compile vs a 30-minute manual build.
4. Include the hard part and one honest reflection ("I'd build the shared infrastructure first"; "I'd compress the libraries, or design standalone from day one") — this is what keeps "proud" from reading as bragging and lands Ownership.
5. Tie it to a Leadership Principle explicitly if asked (Q9 → Ownership; Q8 → Think Big).

**Delivery mechanics:** aim for about two minutes; have the follow-up detail ready (the numbers, the trade-offs, the reflection); speak to ownership and outcome, not the resume. Follow-up questions are where the specific numbers do the work.

**Traps to avoid:** don't tell multiple stories; don't claim team credit (the suite was solo); don't pad with buzzwords; don't skip the reflection; don't confuse "proud" with "technically hardest" — the answer should show judgment and impact, not just difficulty.

## Deep follow-ups (likely probes)

**Q: Which project are you most proud of, and why?**
**A:** The whole suite, because I owned it end to end and it stopped being my project and became how the team worked — that's Q9's answer.

**Q: What specifically within it are you proudest of?**
**A:** The shift from "it works" to "they rely on it." The debug view is what made the team trust the tool for production — owning the outcome, not just the code (Q9 core, para 7).

**Q: What would you do differently?**
**A:** Build the shared infrastructure before the first tool, and collect real floor BOMs before writing the parser — both are in Q9's reflection.

**Q: Can you give another example?**
**A:** Yes — the standalone single-file build in Q8: deleting the server and shipping the suite as one offline HTML file that runs in about 5 seconds.

**Q: What did you personally do versus the team?**
**A:** I was the only engineer on it; that's documented, so I say it plainly. There's no team credit to share or claim (Q9 provenance; Project Context line 4).

**Q: What was the hardest part?**
**A:** PCB vs Layout — the steepest learning curve, because I learned OpenCV.js and built the alignment and parsing side from scratch (Q9 follow-up).
